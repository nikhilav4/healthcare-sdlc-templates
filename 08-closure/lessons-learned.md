# Lessons learned

## When to use it

Run the review after hypercare ends, once people have recovered from go-live but still remember the details, usually within two to four weeks. Invite the whole team and the people who felt the project from outside it: clinical reviewers, super-users, the service desk, the vendor. The point is to change what the next project does, so every lesson should end in a specific change to a template, checklist, estimate, contract or policy, with someone who owns making it. On a long project, hold a short review at each phase gate as well; by closure, most of the early detail is gone.

## Ground rules

- Discuss decisions and the information available when they were made, not the people who made them.
- Work in order: what happened, what it caused, what to change.
- Use a facilitator who wasn't on the project, if you can find one.

Use these to start the discussion and skip any that don't apply.

| Area | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Governance | Were decisions made by the right people, quickly enough? Did escalation work when it was needed? |
| Requirements | What did we learn late that we could have learned early? |
| Vendors | Did vendor commitments hold? What would we write into the contract next time? |
| Testing and UAT | Did clinical reviewers get the time they needed? Did UAT find the problems production later found? |
| Data migration | Which problems surfaced after go-live, and why didn't the test loads catch them? |
| Cutover | How did actual durations compare with the rehearsal? Did downtime procedures work on every unit? |
| Training | Who was missed? How did night and weekend staff fare? |
| Hypercare | Was support in the right places at the right hours? |
