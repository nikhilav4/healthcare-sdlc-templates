# Weekly status report

## When to use it

Send it on the same day every week, from kickoff to closure, to the sponsor, the steering committee and the workstream leads. It should fit on one or two screens and answer three questions for a reader with five minutes: are we on track, what changed, and what do you need from me. Pull the risks and issues straight from the RAID log rather than writing them fresh, so the report and the register never disagree. The same report works in every phase; only the milestones and the areas that matter most change.

## RAG definitions

Agree these with the sponsor at kickoff and keep them in every report, so nobody has to guess what amber means.

| Status | Meaning | What the sponsor should do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green | On track for the committed dates, budget and scope. Known risks are being handled inside the team. | Read the report. Nothing else is needed. |
| Amber | Something committed is at risk, but the team has a recovery plan within its own authority, or the decision it needs is already scheduled. | Watch the recovery plan. Expect a decision request if it hasn't worked by the date given. |
| Red | Something committed will be missed unless someone outside the team acts: a decision, money, people or a vendor escalation. Also red, whatever the schedule says: a patient-safety or privacy problem without an agreed containment. | Act this week on the decisions listed in the report. |

*Overall status is not an average of the areas. The worst area sets it, unless you explain why it shouldn't.*

*Report what you believe, not what avoids a hard conversation. A project that shows green for months and red the week before go-live was amber for a long time and nobody said so.*
